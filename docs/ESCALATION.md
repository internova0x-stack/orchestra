# Escalation and handoff

From `docs/source/7-Escalation-and-Handoff-Playbook.pdf` (Nov 23, 2026). Alexis hands a case to a person
when it reaches the edge of what it may decide, and that person should never need to ask the customer
anything Alexis already knows.

## Triggers

| Trigger | Demo flow | Urgency | What staff do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scam in progress, or someone else is telling the customer what to do | Any | Urgent | Call the customer; consider freezing activity |
| Payment or account on a review hold | Payment on hold | High | Specialist team contacts the customer |
| Recall above the cap | Where is my payout? | High | Supervisor approves or declines the prepared recall |
| Payout past its normal window | Where is my payout? | High | Chase the payment network or receiving bank |
| Unallocated incoming payment matched | Incoming payment | Normal | Confirm the match and credit the account |
| Incoming payment not found, payer's proof supplied | Incoming payment | Normal | Trace from the sending bank's side |
| Bank-stamped document or SWIFT copy requested | Proof of payment | Normal | Produce and send the document |
| Verification past its review window | Business verification | Normal | Check the review and update the customer |
| Fee or rate disputed as wrongly applied | Balances, fees and FX | Normal | Review the charge |
| Records missing or contradictory | Any | Normal | Investigate |
| Identity check failed twice | Any | Normal | Review the account flag; no customer contact needed |
| Formal complaint | Any | Normal | Respond within the complaint timeline |
| Customer asks for a human | Any | Normal, or higher if another trigger applies | Take over the conversation |

More than one trigger: the case takes the highest urgency.

## Response targets

Rules on the Rules screen (`docs/KNOWLEDGE-BASE.md`, "Response targets"). **Owner's values, Nov 23:
Urgent 1 hour, High 4 business hours, Normal 1 business day.** (The playbook's own sample was 15
minutes, 2 business hours, 1 business day; the owner's decision in this session stands.)

Alexis quotes the target as when to expect a response, never when the problem will be solved, and
works the wording out from the rule and the business-hours calendar rather than a fixed phrase: the
playbook's "The team will pick this up today" for High is only true when four business hours remain
today; otherwise it gives the next working day. Outside business hours it says so and gives the next
working day.

## The handoff summary

Written by Alexis through `escalate_to_human`; staff can act on it without opening the transcript.

| Field | Contents |
|---|---|
| Reference | Generated by the tool, e.g. ESC-1042 |
| Urgency | Urgent, high or normal |
| Customer | Business name; the user's name and role; whether identity was verified |
| Request | What the customer asked for, in one sentence |
| What Alexis checked | Each lookup and what it showed, with payment references, amounts and dates |
| What Alexis did | Any action taken, or "none" |
| Why it's handed off | The trigger from the table above |
| What is needed | The specific decision or action staff must take |
| What the customer was told | The expectation Alexis set, so staff don't contradict it |
| Flags | Anything sensitive: a review hold, a suspected scam, a complaint, a distressed customer |

Worked example (recall above the cap): ESC-1042 · High · Brightline Studios Ltd; Ada Okafor, payment
rights; identity verified · Recall a payout sent to the wrong supplier · Payout PO-88213, $14,500.00 by
SWIFT, sent 20 November, status processing; recipient Northgate Supplies · None; recall prepared but not
submitted · Amount is above the $10,000 recall cap · Supervisor to approve or decline the recall · The
recall needs approval, the team will pick it up, recovery is not guaranteed · Flags: none.

Rules: facts only, from the records; no guesses about cause or blame · a review hold says "review hold"
and nothing more · secrets a customer typed are never copied in.

## What the customer is told

Two or three warm, plain sentences saying: it's passing the case to the team and why, in general terms;
the team has the full details, so no repeating; when to expect a response; the reference. Example: "A
recall of this size needs approval from our payments team, Ada, so I've prepared it and passed it to
them with all the details. They'll pick it up today, and your reference is ESC-1042. I should say that a
recall isn't guaranteed once funds have been credited."

Never: promise what the team will decide · give a review hold's reason · ask the customer to contact
support separately (the handoff is the contact) · end the chat abruptly (offer help with anything else).

## The Staff queue (demo)

Case list (open cases newest first: urgency, business name, trigger, age) · case view (the handoff
summary, links to the transcript and activity log) · Approve or Decline a prepared action such as an
over-cap recall (approving runs it and records who approved) · Mark resolved (closes the case with a
short note).

Simplified for the demo: one staff view acting as supervisor, no login (separate agent and supervisor
roles come with staff mode) · no takeover of the live chat · no notifications · no outside ticketing
(the queue is a table in the demo's database).

After the handoff: the case stays open until staff resolve it · if the customer comes back in the same
conversation, Alexis gives the case's status and reference and doesn't open a second case for the same
issue · when staff approve or decline a prepared action, the conversation shows the outcome so the
viewer sees the loop close.

## Build notes

- **Complaints** use `escalate_to_human` with the trigger "formal complaint"; for that trigger the tool
  issues a CMP- reference (the Behaviour Guide's "CMP-0211") instead of ESC-. No extra tool: the demo's
  eleven stay as they are (`log_complaint` is in the backlog).
- **One open case per issue:** `escalate_to_human` returns the existing open case's reference instead of
  opening a second one for the same issue in the same conversation.
